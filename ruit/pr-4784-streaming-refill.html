Two pages on this PR: what changes, then the confirmed custom-policy stall.

A prompt group contains all answers generated for one prompt. The rollout pump produces groups; the train pump consumes them. Dynamic sampling drops a group when all its answers get the same reward and supplies fresh work.

Executed CPU selection example, with two groups required and trainer weight 0. The selection-only fixture assumes C arrived after the earlier replacement decision, so it was unavailable for borrowing at that time. These are group identities, not tensor dimensions.

What the new path changes

The old setup guard allowed dynamic sampling only with exact batch-to-step matching. This PR also opens ready-first and window-based selection. Exact matching keeps each dispatch assigned to a training step; ready-first mixes completed groups across dispatches. Both use the same selection finalizer and the same train pump; group choice and step accounting still differ.

The controller now counts unstamped losses against the step being assembled. It also separates waiting for admission from committing it, letting the rollout pump refill spare prompts while admission waits. That replaces both assumptions behind the earlier restriction; a literal step tag is not required for every policy.

Why keep both?

Exact matching preserves dispatch-to-step membership. Ready-first can use already-completed work from another dispatch, as the example shows. A windowed policy instead uses normal future dispatches and closes at data exhaustion. This tiny example proves selection behavior; it does not establish throughput or convergence.

The in-order-only restriction is removed, but custom compatibility still has a confirmed gap: loading the fixed-weight policy through custom can stall. Comparable GPU measurements remain in the existing evidence thread.
