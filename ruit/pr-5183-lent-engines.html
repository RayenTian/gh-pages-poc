Two pages on this PR, one question each. This one: what the feature does and how it compares with validating inline.

Background: the words on this page In async GRPO, vLLM engines make rollouts all the time while the trainer learns. After each training step a refit copies the new weights to the engines. NeMo Gym is the caller. Validation scores the model on a held-out set. Dotted words have a short explanation: hover or tap them.

The engines to lend are the last num_engines in the list (split_validation_urls). Training requests now go through a small router, so a lent engine can be taken out and put back (setup). Validation gets a second Gym stack that talks to the lent engines directly (init_validation_nemo_gym). A lent engine still joins every refit, but throws the new weights away (_discard_weights_from_collective), so the score belongs to exactly one step's weights.

One validation, step by step

5 engines, 1 lent, validation due at step 8. The lent engine keeps serving training for 3 more steps on its old weights, then serves validation only. When the validation ends, the next refit loads current weights and the engine returns. The score shows up 6 steps after it started, tagged with validation/start_step.

Where the two paths meet, and what still differs

Both call the same function, validate(): inline at L6660, lent through BackgroundValidator at L6616. Scoring and metrics are the same code.

Why keep both, and what lending costs

  • What it buys: in the PR's run (30 steps, a 120B model, 4 engines, 1 lent) the trainer waited 2,201 s on validation inline and 0 s with lending; total time fell from 147.8 to 138.0 min. One run per arm.
  • What it costs: fewer engines make rollouts while a validation runs, scores arrive late, a second Gym stack runs all the time, and all engines switch to vLLM priority scheduling (L1938).
  • Where it works: vLLM engines on their own GPUs, NeMo Gym, in-flight weight updates, and the broadcast refit. Anything else is refused at startup (_validate_validation_engines). Inline stays the default.
So what. The review checked the two things the design rests on, by reading the code: a validation tagged step 8 really scores step-8 weights, and the "drop the weights" flag lands on the same engine workers that take the refit. Both hold. One bug in the new router path stops training for large requests (images): see the second page. Smaller points are in the review comments.
